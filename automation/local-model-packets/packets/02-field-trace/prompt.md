Supplied official-source facts:

- Marathon Texas City, 1987: crane failure damaged an HF vessel and severed
  lines; a sprinkler designed to control an HF release was damaged by the
  crane. About 30,000 to 53,000 pounds of HF were released.
- CITGO Corpus Christi, 2009: a multi-day fire caused multiple failures and an
  HF release. Water supply for fire suppression and HF mitigation was nearly
  exhausted on day one; later saltwater transfer had hose ruptures and pump-
  engine failures. CSB disputed CITGO's capture estimate.
- PES, 2019: ignition-time control communication and later backup power failed;
  a 4:12 a.m. remote water-pump start did not work, and a supervisor manually
  started the supplying pump at about 4:39 a.m.
- The PES report states that water-spray systems were damaged or experienced
  problems in Marathon, CITGO, and PES, and that active safeguards can be
  damaged or unavailable in major fire or explosion events.
- The events have different initiating mechanisms, equipment, inventories,
  timing, water constraints, activation paths, consequence and standing facts,
  and mitigation estimates.

  implementation, comparable denominators, and protection effect remain
  unknown.
