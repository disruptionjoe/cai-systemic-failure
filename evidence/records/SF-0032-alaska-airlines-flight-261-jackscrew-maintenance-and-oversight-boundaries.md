## Event And Scale

Alaska Airlines Flight 261, a McDonnell Douglas MD-83, crashed into the Pacific
Ocean about 2.7 miles north of Anacapa Island. The 2 pilots, 3 cabin
crewmembers, and 83 passengers aboard died.

The event spans component condition, maintenance task and interval, inspection,
wear, trim-system design, flight-crew response, airline maintenance governance,
FAA approval and surveillance, affected standing, and consequence without

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. NTSB identified loss of pitch control
after in-flight failure of the jackscrew assembly's acme-nut threads as probable
cause, insufficient lubrication as the cause of excessive wear, the extended
lubrication and end-play intervals and FAA approvals as contributing, and the
absence of a fail-safe mechanism as separately contributing. This record
preserves those findings without promoting them into recurrence or one
maintenance, inspection, design, certification, surveillance, or oversight
theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between task
performance, intervals, inspection measurement, thread wear, component and
trim-system state, flight-crew response, maintenance-program governance, FAA
surveillance, and fail-safe design. NTSB found weather, dispatch weight and
balance, crew certification and medical condition, grease type, screw surface
finish, foreign debris, and abnormal thread loading were not factors in the
accident or excessive wear as specified in its findings.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a task-performance account, an
interval and detection-opportunity account, a component-wear account, a
single-point design account, a maintenance-program account, and a regulatory-
surveillance account. The source supports relations among them for this event
but not one shared owner, authority, remedy, or recurrence mechanism.
