# SF-0025 - Marshall Pipeline Rupture, Detection, Restart, And Community Boundaries

## Boundary

This is a bounded public-source synthesis of the July 25, 2010 rupture of
Enbridge Line 6B near Marshall, Michigan. It keeps pipe condition and coating,
integrity management, planned shutdown, rupture indications, alarm
interpretation, two later startups, control-center training, public awareness,
emergency response, environmental and community standing, and PHMSA oversight
distinct.

It does not reconstruct complete decision-time custody, alarm interpretation,
accountable review, correction, implementation, relative causal weight,
health causation, remedy effect, one root cause, recurrence, shared authority,
blame, duty, liability, receiver work, or external action.

## Event And Scale

Line 6B ruptured in a wetland during the final minute of a planned shutdown.
The rupture was not discovered or addressed for more than 17 hours. During
that interval Enbridge twice restarted the line and pumped 81 percent of the
estimated 843,444-gallon release after rupture. Oil reached wetlands, Talmadge
Creek, and the Kalamazoo River; residents self-evacuated.

The event spans pipe material, coating, in-line inspection, integrity
management, transient operation, SCADA and leak-detection information,
control-center teamwork, public notification, emergency response, operator,
community, environmental, and regulatory scales without making them one
mechanism.
