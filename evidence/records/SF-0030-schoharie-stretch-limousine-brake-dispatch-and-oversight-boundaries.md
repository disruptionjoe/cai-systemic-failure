## Event And Scale

An altered 2001 Ford Excursion stretch limousine failed to stop at the
NY-30/NY-30A intersection, struck a parked vehicle and two pedestrians, then
continued into an embankment and trees. NTSB estimated the limousine was
traveling 101-118 mph when it struck the parked vehicle. All 18 limousine
occupants and the two pedestrians died.

The event spans vehicle alteration, classification, braking capacity and
condition, maintenance, carrier dispatch, out-of-service enforcement,
operating authority, inspection and registration, repair verification,
occupant protection, affected standing, and state oversight without making
them one mechanism.

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. The controlling NTSB report identifies
the carrier's dispatch of an out-of-service limousine and resulting brake
failure as probable cause, with ineffective NYSDOT oversight and inadequate
repair verification plus inadequate DMV inspection-station oversight and
registration as contributing. This record preserves those findings without
promoting them into recurrence or one maintenance, enforcement, or oversight
theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between
alteration, brake condition, grade demand, carrier dispatch, operating
authority, inspection, registration, repair verification, occupant
protection, and response. NTSB found driver experience, roadway signs, alcohol
or fatigue, cell-phone use, weather, visibility, and stopping sight distance
were not factors; those alternatives remain relevant negative evidence.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a vehicle-maintenance account,
an alteration and classification account, a carrier-dispatch account, an out-
of-service enforcement account, a state-oversight account, and a separate
occupant-protection account. The source supports relations among them for this
event but not one shared owner, authority, remedy, or recurrence mechanism.
