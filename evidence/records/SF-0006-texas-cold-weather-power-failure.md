# SF-0006 - Texas 2021 Cold-Weather Power Failure

## Boundary

This is a bounded public-source synthesis of the February 2021 cold-weather
event in Texas and the South Central United States. It preserves the official
investigators' multi-cause account and asks how prior cold-weather learning,
dependable generation, fuel supply, cross-system coordination, and affected
standing interacted at regional scale.

It does not assign individual blame or legal duty, prescribe electric- or gas-
system remedies, create an accepted topology, or count as a sixth positive
record for `SF-SCHEMA-CANDIDATE-0001`. No confidential material, human or field
research, external contact, deployment, or non-GitHub external action was used.

## Bounded Case

The joint report examines the severe-cold event from 8-20 February 2021. It
reports 1,045 generating units experiencing 4,124 outages, derates, or failures
to start across the study region. ERCOT averaged about 34,000 MW of unavailable
generation for more than two consecutive days. FERC's summary reports that
ERCOT ordered 20,000 MW of rolling blackouts to prevent grid collapse and that
more than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power.

The official account preserves interacting causes. Freezing issues and fuel
issues together accounted for 75.6 percent of unplanned generating-unit
outages, derates, and failures to start. The report attributes some natural-gas
production declines to freezing and some to power losses at gas infrastructure,
which makes gas-electric interdependence visible without reducing the event to
one feedback loop.

## Systemic Trace

- **Information and recurrence:** the official material follows earlier 2011
  and 2018 cold-weather inquiries and again reports familiar freezing-prone
  components and preparation concerns. That sequence establishes recurrent
  official attention, not what any named actor knew or was authorized to do.
- **Resources:** dependable generation was reduced by equipment freezing and
  fuel constraints across multiple fuel types; natural gas was central to most
  fuel-related outages.
- **Authority and coordination:** ERCOT's load-shed decision is a reported
  operational response. The investigators emphasize generator preparedness and
  gas-electric coordination, but the bounded record does not reconstruct every
  authority, delegation, or decision path.
- **Affected standing and scale:** millions of people lost power, and the
  event affected a regional bulk-electric system rather than one facility.
- **Feedback and recovery:** power losses at some gas infrastructure and gas
  supply loss to generators support a possible cross-system feedback. The
  supplied material does not establish that this loop explains every outage or
  how quickly each component recovered.

## Competing Explanations, Counterevidence, Uncertainty, And Falsifiers

Extreme cold, equipment freezing, fuel availability, generator preparation,
planning assumptions, operational constraints, and emergency load shedding
remain distinct interacting explanations. The recurrence of inquiries does not
by itself prove that prior learning was ignored, that a universal mechanism was
present, or that one counterfactual action would have prevented the event.

The source set does not establish individual knowledge, legal duty, complete
decision chronology, the relative contribution of every outage, or a settled
cross-case pattern. It also does not justify treating a necessary emergency
response such as load shedding as the originating failure.

Falsify or narrow the provisional systemic trace if closer source review shows
that earlier inquiries did not preserve relevant cold-weather learning, that
the gas-electric feedback is immaterial to the bounded case, or that the record
cannot keep event severity and narrower component, fuel, planning, or
operational explanations visible without inventing an unsupported common
mechanism.

## Provisional Pattern Hypothesis And Inquiry

A bounded hypothesis is that recurrent stress knowledge can coexist with
insufficient dependable preparation when resource validation and correction
must cross tightly coupled systems. The evidence supports testing that
hypothesis; it does not yet distinguish failure to carry learning from event
severity, implementation limits, fragmented authority, or narrower technical
and resource constraints.

Answerable inquiry: under what evidence and authority conditions does prior
cross-boundary stress knowledge become validated dependable-resource
requirements before an extreme event, rather than remaining advisory learning?
