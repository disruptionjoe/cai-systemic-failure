## Event And Scale

Train 188 entered the Frankford Junction curve, where speed was restricted to
50 mph, at 106 mph. The engineer applied emergency braking as the train entered
the curve, and the locomotive and seven passenger cars derailed seconds later.
Eight passengers died and 185 people were transported to hospitals.

The event spans point-of-operation attention, radio information, curve and
speed rules, signal protection, automatic speed enforcement, braking,
derailment dynamics, railcar containment, passenger standing, mass-casualty
response, hospital distribution, operator responsibility, and federal safety
oversight without making them one mechanism.

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. The controlling NTSB report attributes
probable cause to excessive speed arising from loss of situational awareness,
likely after attention diverted to another train's emergency, and identifies
the lack of PTC as contributing. It separately addresses occupant protection
and patient transport. This record preserves those findings without promoting
them into one recurrence or universal attention or speed-control theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between
required radio monitoring, situational awareness, throttle action, operating
rules, signal design, absent automatic speed enforcement, emergency braking,
railcar containment, and mass-casualty response. The report found no evidence
of fatigue, incapacitation, mobile-phone use, mechanical failure, or ballistic
damage to train 188; those alternatives remain relevant negative evidence.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a point-of-operation awareness
account, a task-management account, a permanent-speed-enforcement account, a
separate occupant-protection account, and a response-coordination account. The
source supports relations among them for this event but not one shared owner,
authority, remedy, or recurrence mechanism.
