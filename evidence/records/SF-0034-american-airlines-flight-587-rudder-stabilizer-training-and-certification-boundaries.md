## Event And Scale

Flight 587, an Airbus A300-605R, crashed into Belle Harbor shortly after
takeoff from John F. Kennedy International Airport. All 260 people aboard and
5 people on the ground died.

The event spans environmental encounter, pilot input, flight-control-system
response, aerodynamic and structural state, training, design, certification,
airline operation, affected standing, and consequence without making them one
mechanism.

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. NTSB identified the in-flight
vertical-stabilizer separation caused by beyond-ultimate-design loads from the
first officer's unnecessary and excessive rudder inputs as probable cause. It
identified A300-600 rudder-system design characteristics and elements of
American Airlines' Advanced Aircraft Maneuvering Program as contributing to
those inputs. This record preserves the report's layered finding without
promoting it into recurrence or one pilot, training, design, certification,
airline, manufacturer, or regulatory theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between the
wake encounter, the first officer's response, alternating inputs, rudder-
system characteristics, load development, certified structural capacity,
stabilizer separation, training, design, certification, standing, and
consequence. The engines separated after the stabilizer and rudder rather than
initiating the loss, and the report treats pilot action, system response, and
training as related but non-identical contributors.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a cue-and-control account, an
aircraft-pilot-coupling account, a rudder-control-design account, a structural-
load account, a training account, and a certification and operational-
guidance account. The source supports relations among them for this event but
not one shared owner, authority, remedy, or recurrence mechanism.
