# SF-0013 - West Fertilizer Storage, Emergency, And Community Boundaries

## Boundary

This is a bounded public-source synthesis of the April 17, 2013 fire and
ammonium-nitrate detonation at the West Fertilizer Company facility in West,
Texas. It keeps fire and detonation, storage and building construction,
emergency preparation, community proximity, regulatory coverage, and insurance
loss-control objects distinct.

It does not determine the fire's origin; reconstruct every person's knowledge,
inspection, information-custody path, or feasible alternative; collapse the
layers into one root cause; determine blame or legal duty; establish recurrence
or fit with the provisional review-chain screen; prescribe a remedy; create
receiver work; or request action.

## Event And Scale

A fire preceded detonation of fertilizer-grade ammonium nitrate stored at the
fertilizer blending, retail, and distribution facility. The event killed 12
emergency responders and three members of the public, injured more than 260
people, destroyed the facility, and damaged more than 150 offsite buildings,
including residences, schools, apartments, and a nursing home. The record spans
storage equipment, facility construction, emergency response, insurance,
regulation, land use, and affected-community scales without treating them as
one mechanism.

## System Interaction Lenses

  land-use knowledge, and emergency information existed at different times;
  complete custody and accountable disposition remain unknown.
- **Resources:** storage construction, sprinklers, detection, training,
  firefighting, evacuation, insurance, inspection, and land-use controls were
  distinct resources rather than one protective layer.
- **Authority:** facility management, insurers, volunteer fire services,
  federal and state agencies, and local land-use authorities occupied different
  decision surfaces whose full transitions are not reconstructed.
- **Power:** storage and development choices shaped responder and community
  exposure without proving a generalized power mechanism.
- **Participation and standing:** responders, workers, students, residents,
  nursing-home occupants, and other community members carried distinct affected
  standing; their complete warning and correction routes remain unknown.
- **Scale:** material, bin, building, facility, insurer, fire department,
  regulator, municipality, and community interacted without establishing
  cross-case recurrence.
- **Feedback and adaptation:** insurer surveys, hazard reporting, drills,
  inspections, and earlier FGAN incidents were distinct feedback objects; exact
  receipt and closure decisions are incomplete.
