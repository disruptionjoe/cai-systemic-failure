## Event And Scale

Inbound train 112 struck the rear of stopped inbound train 214 on the Red Line
near Fort Totten. The lead car of train 112 overrode and telescoped with the
rear car of train 214, causing a major loss of occupant survival space. Nine
people aboard train 112, including its operator, died, and emergency responders
transported 52 people to hospitals.

The event spans physical track-circuit modules, signal detection, automatic
train control, speed commands, testing, maintenance, operator safety
management, board and regulatory oversight, emergency response, railcar
structure, and passenger standing without making them one mechanism.

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. The controlling NTSB report attributes
the accident's probable cause to the failed track-circuit modules and WMATA's
failure to institutionalize the enhanced verification test. It separately
identifies maintenance, safety culture, governance, regulatory authority, and
crashworthiness contributions. This record preserves those distinctions
rather than promoting them into one recurrence or universal control theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between
physical module oscillation, signal interpretation, verification-test
institutionalization, maintenance planning, automatic speed commands,
operator action, oversight authority, and railcar survival performance. The
report also rejected the mixed-manufacturer impedance-bond configuration as
the cause absent parasitic oscillation in the GRS modules.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a component-failure account, a
test-implementation account, a maintenance-and-monitoring account, an operator
safety-management account, an oversight-authority account, and a separate
injury-severity account. The source supports relations among them for this
accident but does not establish recurrence across rail systems or other
domains.
