## Event And Scale

Stretch Duck 7, a 33-foot modified World War II-era DUKW amphibious passenger
vessel, sank during rapidly moving severe weather. Of 31 people aboard, 17
died and 14 were rescued. NTSB found the emergency response timely and
effective.

The event spans weather information, tour operations, vessel configuration,
flooding, buoyancy, passenger egress, company oversight, Coast Guard
oversight, affected standing, and response without making them one mechanism.

## Claim Status, Counterevidence, And Alternatives

Claim status: `investigation_synthesis`. NTSB identified continued waterborne
operation after the severe thunderstorm warning as probable cause, waves
entering the non-weathertight bow hatch as the flooding path, insufficient
reserve buoyancy as contributing to the sinking, and ineffective action on
fixed-canopy emergency egress as contributing to loss of life. This record
preserves those findings without promoting them into recurrence or one
weather, operation, vessel-design, survivability, or oversight theory.

Counterevidence to one complete mechanism includes the separation between
forecasting, company receipt and monitoring, manager sequencing, captain
operation, hatch condition, buoyancy, egress, lifejackets, response, and Coast
Guard oversight. NTSB found propulsion, steering, bilge systems, alcohol, and
impairing drugs were not factors; Stretch Duck 54 exited under the same storm
with greater freeboard, reserve buoyancy, and a securable bow hatch.

Alternative bounded explanations remain live: a weather-use and operating-
decision account, a flooding-path account, a reserve-buoyancy account, an
emergency-egress account, and separate company and Coast Guard oversight
accounts. The source supports relations among them for this event but not one
shared owner, authority, remedy, or recurrence mechanism.
