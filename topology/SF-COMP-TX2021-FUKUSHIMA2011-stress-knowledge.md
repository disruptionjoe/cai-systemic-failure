# Texas 2021 / Fukushima 2011 Stress-Knowledge Comparison

## Boundary

This comparison tests whether two unlike severe events support a recurring
failure to carry prior stress knowledge into validated dependable preparation,
or only show different forms of prior-hazard attention. It uses the official-
source material already bounded in `SF-0006` and `SF-MIX-0001`.

It does not merge the cases, establish a common cause, accept a topology,
assign individual blame or legal duty, prescribe a remedy, make a nuclear-
safety or electric-reliability finding, create receiver work, or authorize
external action.

## Source Set

- Texas: FERC, NERC, and Regional Entity staff, "The February 2021 Cold
  Weather Outages in Texas and the South Central United States," dated 8
  December 2021, plus FERC's final-report summary, linked in `SF-0006`.
- Fukushima: IAEA Director General report and Technical Volume 2, plus the
  official-archive entry point for the National Diet inquiry, linked in
  `SF-MIX-0001`.

## Supported Case Traces

Texas preserves recurrent official cold-weather attention, familiar freezing-
prone components, equipment freezing, fuel constraints, gas-electric
interdependence, rolling load shedding, and regional affected standing. The
bounded evidence does not reconstruct every actor's knowledge, authority, or
decision chronology and does not make gas-electric feedback the whole event.

Fukushima preserves a licensing design basis grounded partly in historical
tsunami records, later reassessment and underestimation pressure, loss of
safety functions, beyond-design-basis accident-management gaps, and visible
operator, regulator, legal-framework, site-protection, and emergency-response
paths. The bounded evidence does not supply a complete decision chronology or
authority map and keeps the case mixed rather than counting it as a sixth
positive record.

| Dimension | Texas, February 2021 | Fukushima Daiichi, March 2011 |
|---|---|---|

## Shared Residue And Material Differences

The sources directly support only a narrow shared residue: each case contains
officially visible stress knowledge relevant to dependable preparation before
or around a later severe event, and each exposes a validation question that
cannot be reduced to event severity alone. Texas concerns weatherized power
and fuel resources across coupled infrastructures. Fukushima concerns external
hazard assessment, a nuclear licensing design basis, site protection, safety
functions, and accident management. Neither case supplies missing facts for
the other.

## Knowledge-To-Validation Test

The comparison supports a provisional recurring inquiry, not a common causal
finding. The exact link that remains unproved is that, in both cases, prior
stress knowledge reached an actor with decision authority in a form that
materially changed the current validation burden, yet the actor failed to
close that burden before the event.

Without that decision-time chain, the evidence may show only two different
forms of prior-hazard attention followed by severe events. Recurrent attention,
later reassessment, or a visible gap after failure does not by itself prove
operative reuse, accountable review, validation failure, or one cross-domain
mechanism.

## Competing Explanations, Counterevidence, And Unknowns

Texas remains explainable through interacting weather, equipment, fuel,
planning, coordination, market, and operating constraints. Fukushima remains
explainable through earthquake and tsunami hazard, design basis, site
protection, nuclear safety, regulation, emergency response, and accident-
management limits. The source sets do not establish equivalent authorities,
validation methods, stop conditions, or counterfactual remedies.

Falsify or narrow the proposed recurring mechanism if closer evidence cannot
show, separately in each case, the relevant knowledge object, its decision-
time recipient and authority, the changed validation burden, and the unresolved
condition that remained before the event. Also narrow it if the strongest
supported account in either case is fully absorbed by its domain-specific
hazard, resource, design, or operating explanation.

## Result

The comparison preserves a source-backed cross-domain question while refusing
a common causal conclusion. It creates no schema, remedy, acceptance,
receiver-work, external-action, nuclear-safety, emergency-response, health,
legal, electric-reliability, or regulatory change.
