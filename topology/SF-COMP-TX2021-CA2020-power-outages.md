# Texas 2021 / California 2020 Power-Outage Comparison

## Boundary

This comparison tests whether two extreme-weather power failures support a
shared systemic pattern or only a surface resemblance. It uses `SF-0006` and
the official-source material already preserved in
`SF-ABS-0007-resource-adequacy-rolling-outages.md`.

It does not make California a review-chain positive, use one case to fill the
other's evidence gaps, prescribe a power-system remedy, accept a schema, or
claim that extreme-weather outages have one universal cause.

## Source Set

- Texas: the FERC, NERC, and Regional Entity staff final report dated 8
  December 2021 and FERC's 16 November 2021 final-report summary, linked in
  `evidence/records/SF-0006-texas-cold-weather-power-failure.md`.
- California: CAISO, CPUC, and CEC, "Final Root Cause Analysis: Mid-August
  2020 Extreme Heat Wave," 13 January 2021, plus the CPUC final-report page,
  linked in `SF-ABS-0007-resource-adequacy-rolling-outages.md`.

## Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Texas, February 2021 | California, August 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| Initiating stress | Severe cold and freezing precipitation. | West-wide extreme heat and demand beyond planning targets. |
| Resource loss or inadequacy | Unit outages, derates, failures to start, component freezing, and fuel constraints. | Insufficient dependable early-evening supply under resource-adequacy and planning processes. |
| Prior information | Earlier cold-weather inquiries and repeated preparation concerns are explicit. | Existing planning targets are explicit; the bounded source set here does not establish a comparable warning lineage. |
| Planning and validation | Cold-weather preparation and gas-electric coordination are investigator concerns. | Planning targets, resource adequacy, and dependable early-evening supply are central findings. |
| Interdependence | Gas production and electric power are coupled in both directions for part of the event. | Imports, the wider Western heat event, net-load timing, and day-ahead market practices interact; gas-electric coupling is not supplied as the central account. |
| Operational response | ERCOT ordered rolling blackouts to prevent grid collapse. | CAISO instituted rotating outages on 14 and 15 August. |
| Affected standing | More than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power. | Electricity customers experienced rotating outages; the bounded sources supplied here do not quantify people affected. |
| Scale | Texas and the wider South Central study region. | California within a West-wide heat event and regional market context. |
| Feedback and recurrence | Prior cold-weather inquiries and a possible gas-electric feedback are visible. | No single root cause; the bounded evidence centers extreme weather, resource adequacy, planning, and market practice. |

## Shared-Pattern Test

Both cases directly support a narrow residue: extreme weather exposed a gap
between stressed conditions and dependable electricity resources, and system
operators used controlled outages as an emergency response. That is more
specific than "bad weather caused blackouts," but it does not yet prove one
recurring knowledge-to-validation or cross-boundary correction mechanism.

Texas supplies recurrence and gas-electric coupling evidence that the bounded
California source set does not. California supplies explicit resource-
adequacy, early-evening supply, and market-practice findings that should not be
projected onto Texas. The stronger claim that anticipated stress was known yet
failed to become validated dependable-resource requirements across boundaries
therefore remains an inquiry, not a comparison result.

## Difference And Absorber Test

California remains the resource-adequacy and capacity-tradeoff absorber recorded
by `SF-ABS-0007`; this comparison does not convert it into positive evidence for
the review-chain candidate. Texas is also not admitted as a sixth review-chain
positive. Component freezing, fuel supply, and gas-electric coupling may
explain Texas more narrowly, while heat-driven net demand, dependable early-
evening capacity, imports, reserves, and market practices may explain
California more narrowly.

## Counterevidence, Unknowns, And Falsifier

The bounded source sets do not establish equivalent planning authorities,
warning histories, validation procedures, market designs, or correction
routes. They do not show that the same intervention would work in both cases.
Falsify the proposed stronger pattern if closer evidence shows that the only
commonality is weather-driven scarcity, or that the relevant prior knowledge,
authority, validation burden, and correction path cannot be aligned without
using one case to supply missing facts for the other.

## Answerable Inquiry

Across distinct extreme-weather electricity failures, what evidence shows that
anticipated stress was translated—or failed to be translated—into validated
dependable-resource requirements across organizational and system boundaries,
and which narrower technical, resource, market, or event-severity explanations
absorb that mechanism in each case?

This is a candidate Mechanism Design inquiry only. It authorizes no receiver
work, intervention selection, field research, public recommendation, or
external action.
