# Flint / Grenfell Affected-Observation Comparison

## Boundary

This comparison tests whether the bounded Flint and Grenfell records support a
recurring failure to preserve affected-party observations as accountable
validation objects, or only two domain-specific warning histories. It uses the
official-source material already bounded in `SF-0003` and `SF-0005`.

It does not equate complaints, sampling, resident concerns, professional or
coroner warnings, design review, product assurance, building control, or fire-
service evidence. It assigns no blame or legal duty, prescribes no remedy,
accepts no schema, creates no receiver work, and authorizes no external action.

## Source Set

- Flint: the Flint Water Advisory Task Force final report, EPA OIG report,
  EPA emergency order, CDC report, and Michigan Civil Rights Commission report
  linked in `SF-0003`.
- Grenfell: the Grenfell Tower Inquiry Phase 1 and Phase 2 reports and the
  Independent Review of Building Regulations and Fire Safety linked in
  `SF-0005`.

## Supported Case Traces

Flint preserves a changed water source, altered corrosion-control and exposure
validation burdens, resident complaints, independent sampling, official
concern, later health evidence, emergency action, and distinct utility, state,
and federal paths. Residents carry affected standing; utility and oversight
institutions are evidence custodians, review recipients, or authority holders,
not substitutes for that standing.

Grenfell preserves a refurbishment that changed the external-wall, window,
product, design, and fire-safety evidence context. Resident safety concerns sit
beside—not in place of—prior-incident learning, professional or coroner
concerns, design and building-control review, product assurance, fire-service
preparedness, and government guidance. Residents, visitors, responders, and
nearby communities carry affected standing while review crosses many distinct
owners and authorities.

## Observation-To-Validation Test

The comparison supports one narrow shared residue: affected-party observations
were visible inside changed validation contexts, while custody, review,
authority, and correction crossed institutional boundaries. It does not yet
show that either observation set was preserved as the same kind of object or
that one recurring mechanism explains both cases.

The exact common link that remains unproved is that, before the relevant
decision or exposure became final, a source-preserved affected-party
observation reached a recipient able to connect it to a named current
validation burden and keep that burden unresolved until an accountable owner
dispositioned it.

## Competing Explanations, Counterevidence, And Unknowns

Flint may remain primarily a drinking-water treatment, sampling, public-health,
civil-rights, oversight, or emergency-authority failure. Grenfell may remain
primarily a product, facade, construction, building-control, regulatory, or
emergency-response failure. Institutional plurality, warning presence, delayed
response, and severe harm are insufficient to establish a common mechanism.

The bounded records do not reconstruct every observation's time, custodian,
recipient, authority, decision relevance, or disposition. They also do not
show that resident evidence was equivalent to technical, professional,
regulatory, or incident-learning evidence.

Falsify or narrow the shared residue if closer evidence shows that the relevant
affected-party observations were timely preserved, connected to the then-
current validation burden, and dispositioned by an accountable owner; if the
chains cannot be reconstructed without borrowing facts across cases; or if one
generic observation object erases source type, standing, authority, timing, or
the stronger domain explanation.

## Result

The comparison adds a source-preserving test of affected-observation custody
while refusing a common causal conclusion. It creates no schema, remedy,
acceptance, receiver-work, external-action, water, health, civil-rights,
building-safety, fire-service, regulatory, or legal change.
