# Texas 2021 / I-35W Stress-Knowledge Absorber Test

## Boundary

This comparison tests whether recurrent Texas cold-weather attention and the
I-35W design/load history support one failure to translate stress knowledge
into current validation, or whether the bridge-capacity account demonstrates
why prior attention plus severe outcome is insufficient. It uses the public
official sources already bounded in `SF-0006` and `SF-ABS-0006`.

It does not convert either case into schema evidence, assign blame or legal
duty, prescribe electric, gas, bridge, engineering, inspection, or regulatory
remedies, create receiver work, or authorize external action.

## Supported Case Traces

Texas preserves severe cold, freezing and fuel constraints, recurrent 2011 and
2018 cold-weather inquiries, familiar component and preparation concerns,
rolling load shedding, regional affected standing, and a possible gas-electric
feedback. The bounded evidence does not make that feedback the whole event or
reconstruct every actor's knowledge, authority, or decision chronology.

I-35W preserves inadequate gusset-plate capacity from original design error,
added loads, construction equipment and aggregate staging, load-rating scope,
inspection guidance, quality control, owner review, standards guidance, and
contractor load placement. The existing absorber holds these as a narrower
structural-capacity and design-calculation account rather than positive review-
chain evidence.

## Tempting Surface Fit And Material Differences

Both cases combine earlier knowledge, changed stress or load, many actors,
visible validation practices, severe consequence, and later correction routes.
That surface resemblance does not identify the same knowledge object,
recipient, authority, current burden, unresolved condition, or failure mode.

Texas concerns recurrent environmental stress across tightly coupled resource
systems. I-35W concerns an inadequate structural component under design and
load history. Multi-actor review and severe outcome do not bridge those domain
differences.

## Stress-Knowledge Test And Absorber Result

The I-35W account absorbs a generic shared mechanism. Prior attention plus a
later severe event is insufficient when the evidence does not independently
show, in each case, the same causal chain from a source-preserved stress
knowledge object through a decision-time accountable recipient to a current
validation burden that remained improperly open or was incorrectly closed.

Texas retains a provisional knowledge-to-validation question. I-35W remains a
negative absorber for the review-chain candidate and demonstrates why design-
calculation, structural-capacity, load-rating, inspection, and construction-
load evidence must not be relabeled as a generic stress-knowledge failure.

## Counterevidence, Unknowns, And Falsifier

The bounded sources do not establish equivalent knowledge objects, validation
methods, authority paths, stop conditions, or counterfactual remedies. They do
not show that gas-electric feedback corresponds to bridge load rating, or that
original design error corresponds to cold-weather preparation; those direct
cross-domain links would be category errors rather than required evidence.

A shared mechanism could survive the absorber only if closer source evidence
independently reconstructs in both cases: the relevant pre-event knowledge
object; its custody and revision; a decision-time recipient with authority; the
current stress-specific validation burden; its disposition or unresolved stop
condition; and a causal link between that disposition and the later failure.

Falsify or narrow this absorber result if that full chain is supported in both
cases without erasing the electric/gas and structural-capacity explanations.
Strengthen the absorber if either chain depends only on hindsight, institutional
plurality, prior attention, or outcome severity.

## Result

The comparison preserves the Texas inquiry and the I-35W absorber while
refusing a shared causal finding. It creates no schema, remedy, acceptance,
receiver-work, external-action, electric-reliability, gas-system, bridge-
safety, engineering, transportation, regulatory, or legal change.
