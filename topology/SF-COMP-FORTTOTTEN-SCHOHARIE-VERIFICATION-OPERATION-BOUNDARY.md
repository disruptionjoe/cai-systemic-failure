# Fort Totten And Schoharie Verification-To-Operation Boundary

## Boundary

This comparison tests only a bounded relation between evidence that an adverse
condition existed, a control capable of identifying or stopping it, the control
actually used, and later operation. It uses `NTSB/RAR-10/02`,
`NTSB/HAR-20/03`, `SF-0028`, `SF-0030`, and
`SF-FORT-TOTTEN-TEST-DETECTION-PARTIAL-TRACE-0001`.

Fort Totten concerns a rail track-circuit fault, an enhanced verification test
that was not institutionalized or used systemwide, a different field
verification, a false-vacant indication, and automatic speed commands.
Schoharie concerns a highway vehicle placed out of service, unverified repair,
missing operating authority, carrier dispatch, ineffective enforcement and
oversight, brake condition, and operation. A capable test and an out-of-service
order are different control objects. Similarity at the verification-to-
operation level does not establish recurrence, one mechanism, or shared
authority.

## Decision, Counterevidence, And Falsifiers

Decision: `preserve_separate_verification_operation_boundaries`.

The reports support a bounded comparison of adverse-condition evidence,
verification or stop controls, later operational state, and affected standing.
They do not support one verification-to-operation mechanism. Rail component
behavior, field testing, automatic commands, highway vehicle condition,
repair verification, carrier dispatch, operating authority, and enforcement
are load-bearing counterevidence to collapsing the cases.

Falsify or materially narrow this comparison if closer official-source review
shows that the enhanced Fort Totten test would not have identified the faulty
circuit, that the Schoharie limousine was not under an out-of-service order or
did not remain unverified, or that the later speed-command or dispatch
relations did not occur. Reject any stronger chain that fills complete
custody, accountable receipt, authority, disposition, correction,
implementation, causal-weight, recurrence, or control-effect gaps.
